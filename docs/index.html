Installation notes & permissions

Windows

SmartScreen may show an “unknown publisher” warning on first run — choose More info → Run anyway.

macOS

The app isn't notarized yet, so Gatekeeper will block it the first time —
1. right-click the app and choose Open (Drag to Applications directory)
2. Run xattr -cr /Applications/Reflectodoro.app in Terminal.

Linux

Make the AppImage executable first (chmod +x Reflectodoro*.AppImage); some distros also need libfuse2 installed for it to run.

Android

You'll need to allow “install from unknown sources” for your browser or file manager, and Play Protect may warn about an unrecognized developer (there's no Play Store listing yet).

For reliable break enforcement in the background, grant Display over other apps, Notifications and Alarms & reminders when onboarding asks, and disable battery optimization for Reflectodoro in Settings — on Xiaomi/Oppo/Vivo phones, also enable its “auto-start” toggle. Otherwise some OEMs can kill the background timer.

Build it yourself

If a prebuilt binary doesn't work on your platform, follow the Prerequisites and Build a production installer instructions in the README.

How it works

Four steps, on repeat, all day.

No configuration ritual, no streaks to protect. Install it and the clock does the rest.

1

Work on the wall clock

Slots are pinned to real clock boundaries — :00–:25 work, :25–:30 break.

:00 – :25 WORK BREAK :30 – :55 WORK BREAK
2

The break screen takes over

A fullscreen, always-on-top window appears. The close button is intercepted, and on Windows and Linux/X11 even Alt+Tab is suppressed while it's up.

Alt + F4 Alt + Tab Win
3

Reflect your way out

Write what you actually did. In a hurry? Type the random on-screen code to end the break early — you still have to write the reflection.

time up + reflection = unlock
4

Wellness check-in

Quick toggle whether you completed intended tasks in break — Water, Exercise, Relax eyes.

Why it helps

Forced breaks aren't a gimmick.

Health benefit 🧡

Regular, enforced breaks cut down on eye strain, stiffness, and the posture damage of sitting through hours of uninterrupted screen time.

▾
📖

BookLong, uninterrupted sitting harms the body no matter how much you exercise elsewhere — what matters is breaking it up regularly (James Levine, Get Up!).

🔬

ResearchEvery 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds to reduce digital eye strain (American Optometric Association's “20-20-20” rule).

Mental refresh 🌿

Stepping away on a fixed clock — not “just one more thing” — keeps focus fatigue from building up across the day.

▾
📖

BookDeliberate, scheduled rest is what makes deep, sustained focus possible — not productivity's enemy, but its precondition (Alex Soojung-Kim Pang, Rest).

🔬

ResearchThe most productive users work in focused ~52-minute bursts, then break for ~17 minutes (2014 DeskTime work-tracking analysis).

Rethink & reset 💡

Writing what you just did forces a quick gut-check on whether it's still the right next step, instead of coasting on autopilot. It also help come out of droom-scrolling.

▾
📖

Book“Reflection-in-action” — pausing mid-task to think and adjust course — is what separates deliberate work from autopilot (Donald Schön, The Reflective Practitioner).

Builds a real record 📜

Every reflection is saved, so you end the day with an honest, timestamped log of what you actually worked on — not a guess.

▾
📖

BookA brief, honest daily log beats relying on memory to know where your time actually went (Ryder Carroll, The Bullet Journal Method).

🔬

ResearchMemory of how you spent your day is unreliable unless reconstructed close in time to the events — a log kept in the moment beats one recalled later (Kahneman, Krueger, Schkade, Schwarz & Stone, Science, 2004).

Fights burnout 💥

The wellness check nudges habits (water, exercise, movement) that are easy to skip when you're heads-down, before they add up.

▾
📖

BookStress cycle needs to be actively “completed” through small physical actions, not just removing the stressor, or it accumulates (Emily & Amelia Nagoski, Burnout).

Sharper prioritization 🎯

The Most Important Tasks list keeps today's real priorities in view, so breaks don't derail you — they refocus you without mental stress.

▾
📖

BookNaming your single most important task keeps ordinary busyness from crowding out what actually matters (Gary Keller, The One Thing).

FAQ

Questions people actually ask

Especially about getting out of a break screen that won't budge.

The break screen won't close — how do I get out?

The normal way out is writing your reflection, then either waiting out the timer or typing the on-screen emergency-exit code — both still require the reflection to be submitted.

If it's genuinely stuck (a bug, not the enforcement working as intended), keep in mind the break screen is fullscreen and always-on-top — you generally can't click through to the taskbar, tray, or Task Manager to close it that way, so use one of these instead:

  • Windows / Linux — press Ctrl+Alt+Shift+F12 to instantly force-destroy the break screen. This is the reliable escape hatch on these platforms.
  • macOS — press Cmd+Option+Shift+F12.
  • Android — there's no keyboard shortcut equivalent. Go to Settings → Apps → Reflectodoro and either revoke “Display over other apps” or force-stop the app.
  • Any platform, last resort — restart your PC or phone. If the overlay stops you reaching the normal restart menu, hold the physical power button down for several seconds (on a PC, this force-shuts-it-down; press it again to power back on) to force a restart.

A dev build additionally shows a visible “Close (DEV)” button on the break screen.

Can I still answer a phone call while the break screen is up on Android?

Yes. Android's break screen is a system overlay drawn on top of other apps, not a lock screen, so incoming-call UI and the notification shade still work over it. If the call's own full-screen UI doesn't pop up automatically, pull down the notification bar from the top of the screen and answer from there — the break screen resumes underneath once you're done.

How do I contact someone for help?

Join the Discord community — that's the fastest way to reach the developer and other users. For bugs or feature requests you can also open an issue on GitHub. Log file can be downloaded from About tab.

What happens if I don't enter a reflection before the break timer runs out?

The break screen simply stays up. Unlocking always requires a submitted reflection — the timer running out by itself never ends the break. In formula terms: (time expired AND reflection entered) OR (challenge typed AND reflection entered). So a break can run well past its nominal 5 minutes if you just don't write anything.

Does the break screen eventually auto-close on its own in that case?

Yes, as a backstop for a genuinely stuck screen — after a configurable grace period (Settings → break screen auto-close, 1–60 minutes) it force-closes itself, the same as using a kill switch: no reflection is recorded and no wellness check-in opens.

On Android, the native overlay also carries its own independent 20-minute watchdog as a second layer, in case the usual auto-close signal never reaches it.

Can I skip the reflection using the emergency-exit challenge?

No. Typing the challenge only replaces waiting out the timer — it's an early exit from the time requirement, not the reflection requirement. You still have to write and submit what you did either way.

Is my data private? Does Reflectodoro sync across devices?

Everything is stored locally in a SQLite file on your device — there's no account, no server, and nothing is uploaded anywhere. Cross-device sync is on the roadmap but not built yet, so today each install's history is fully independent.

Can I change the work/break lengths from 25 and 5 minutes?

Not yet — they're fixed constants for now. Configurable durations are a planned addition, not currently exposed in Settings.

Why does macOS block the app with a Gatekeeper warning on first launch?

The macOS build isn't code-signed or notarized yet (no Apple Developer account). Right-click the app and choose Open, or run xattr -cr /Applications/Reflectodoro.app in Terminal to clear the quarantine flag.

Why does Android show a persistent alarm-clock icon in the status bar?

That's Android itself, not a bug: reliable break scheduling uses AlarmManager.setAlarmClock, which is the only alarm API exempt from Android's background-launch restrictions. The OS shows that icon for any app using it, for as long as Pomodoro mode is on.

What permissions does the Android app need, and why?
  • Display over other apps — draws the break screen on top of whatever you're using.
  • Notifications — shows the break notification, and a fallback if the overlay permission isn't granted.
  • Alarms & reminders — needed for exact, on-time break scheduling.

Onboarding and Settings also recommend disabling battery optimization for Reflectodoro (and enabling “auto-start” on Xiaomi/Oppo/Vivo phones) — some OEMs otherwise kill the background timer outright.

Why is Reflectodoro free?

Reflectodoro is in early release, and every feature it has today is free. Premium features are planned but not built yet — nothing currently in the app is being held back or paywalled.
